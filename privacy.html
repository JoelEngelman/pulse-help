Pulse · Privacy & Safety

Privacy comes first.

This page explains the privacy protections currently built into Pulse, what information the app needs to work, how school hours are handled, and what you should still do to keep your account safe.

What Pulse does for privacy

Pulse is designed so that useful features do not require making everything public.

🔒

School-hours privacy screen

On weekdays, Pulse shows a privacy screen during school hours. The current gate is 9:00 AM–3:30 PM. It is based on the device's local time and does not check which Wi-Fi network you are using.

👤

No public user directory

Pulse's safety page states that Pulse does not publish a public directory of everyone using the service. Searching for someone does not give permission to repeatedly contact or harass them.

🛡️

Authentication is protected

Pulse uses an access token for authenticated API requests. The browser sends that token as a Bearer token when it needs to access protected Pulse features.

🌐

HTTPS API connection

The browser connects to Pulse's API over HTTPS. Requests use CORS and omit browser credentials such as cookies from the fetch layer.

💾

Some browser-only data

Pulse stores some preferences and local app data in your browser, such as theme settings, saved account names, dismissed notices, some achievement data and locally stored Pulse AI chats. This data is not the same thing as a public Pulse profile.

🚫

Blocking & safety controls

Pulse includes blocking and safety controls so users can manage unwanted contact. The Safety & Privacy area explains the available controls.

What information Pulse needs

Some information has to exist for a social app to function. The important part is understanding where it goes and why it exists.

Account informationYour account needs identifying account information such as a username and profile information. Your password is used for authentication and should never be shared with another person.
Messages and conversationsMessages need to be stored by the Pulse backend so the intended participants can retrieve their conversations. Do not put secrets, passwords, addresses or other highly sensitive information into messages.
Profile informationYour display name, bio and avatar are profile information. Only put information in your profile that you are comfortable sharing with the people who can see your profile.
Presence and activityPulse can send heartbeat/presence information while you are signed in so features such as online status can work. The app also records some activity used for achievements and account features.

What stays in your browser

Pulse uses browser storage for several convenience features.

  • Your Pulse access token and refresh token are stored in browser local storage so the app can keep you signed in.
  • Saved account names can be stored locally to make switching/signing in easier.
  • Your selected theme and some interface preferences are stored locally.
  • Pulse AI chat history is stored locally in the browser for the AI page.
  • Some achievement, avatar-history and creator-draft information is stored locally.
Shared computer warningIf you use Pulse on a school, library or family computer, sign out when you finish. Browser storage can remain on that device even after you close the tab.

School & student safety

Pulse is intended to respect school privacy rather than help students bypass school controls.

🏫

During school

The privacy screen is intended to prevent Pulse from being used during the configured weekday school window. It is a privacy feature, not a school-network bypass.

📚

School networks

Schools can filter websites and network traffic. Pulse does not attempt to bypass those controls. If a school network blocks Pulse, the correct path is to ask the school's IT or administration team.

🤝

Respect other students

Do not use Pulse to pressure, bully, threaten, impersonate or repeatedly contact another student after they have asked you to stop.

🧑‍🏫

Adults & school staff

If there is a serious safety issue involving a student, tell a trusted adult, parent/guardian, teacher or school wellbeing staff member. Pulse is not a replacement for immediate real-world help.

Important: Never use a VPN, proxy or other method to bypass school access controls. Network administrators decide what is permitted on a managed school network.

How safe is Pulse?

Pulse has privacy and safety protections, but no online service can honestly promise that it is completely risk-free.

  • Use a unique, strong password.
  • Never share your password or authentication information.
  • Check that you are messaging the correct account before sending something private.
  • Do not share your home address, phone number, passwords, school login, payment details or other sensitive information in chats.
  • Sign out on shared computers.
  • Block or report people who make you uncomfortable.
  • Tell a trusted adult if something online makes you feel unsafe.

One important limitation

The school-hours privacy screen is a client-side feature. Because it uses the browser's local clock, someone with control of their device can potentially change the device time and get around the screen. It should therefore be understood as a privacy measure, not a tamper-proof school access-control system.

Why this is still usefulThe goal is to make Pulse unavailable during ordinary school use without collecting your Wi-Fi/network identity. Pulse deliberately does not need to know which school network you are on for this feature.
