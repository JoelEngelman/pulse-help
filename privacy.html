Pulse · Privacy & School Safety

Privacy comes first.

This is the detailed explanation of the privacy, safety and school-related protections currently built into Pulse — including what information the app uses, what stays in your browser, what your privacy controls actually do, and what the school-hours screen can and cannot protect.

At a glance

Pulse has several privacy protections, but no online service is completely risk-free.

🔒

School-hours privacy screen

Pulse currently blocks the app on weekdays from 8:30 AM to 3:30 PM. This is a temporary testing time and can be returned to 9:00 AM. The check uses the device's local clock only.

🌐

No school Wi-Fi tracking

The school-hours feature does not identify your Wi-Fi network, school network or location. It deliberately works without checking which network you are connected to.

👤

Discoverability control

You can turn off Appear in people search. The user-search endpoint only returns accounts marked discoverable.

💬

Search-only messaging

You can enable a setting that requires people to find you through search before starting a chat. This gives you more control over who can initiate conversations.

🚫

Blocking

Pulse includes blocked-account controls so you can manage unwanted contact.

🗑️

Account deletion

The account settings include a permanent account-deletion action. You must type DELETE to confirm it.

What data Pulse uses

Social features need some information to function. Here is what the current app and API are designed around.

Account informationPulse needs an account with information such as a username and profile information. Profile fields can include a display name, bio and avatar.
AuthenticationAfter sign-in, the browser receives an access token and may receive a refresh token. The current web client stores these in browser local storage so the session can continue. Authenticated API requests use the access token as a Bearer token.
MessagesMessages and conversations have to be handled by the Pulse backend so participants can retrieve them. Treat private messages as private communication, not as a place to store passwords, school credentials, addresses or other secrets.
PresenceWhile signed in, Pulse can send a heartbeat so the service can know that an account is online and update its last-seen information.
Privacy settingsYour account stores privacy choices such as whether you are discoverable and whether messaging should be search-only.

What happens in your browser

Some information is stored locally on the device you are using.

  • The Pulse access token is stored in browser local storage under the Pulse token key.
  • A refresh token may also be stored locally when the authentication service provides one.
  • Saved account names can be stored locally to make account switching easier.
  • Theme and interface preferences are stored locally.
  • Some Pulse features store app-specific information locally, including certain achievement, avatar-history, creator-draft and AI-chat data.
Shared-device warningClosing a tab is not the same as signing out. If you use Pulse on a school, library or family computer, sign out when finished because browser storage can remain on that device.

How Pulse talks to its backend

The web client sends API requests over HTTPS to the Pulse API service.

🔐

HTTPS

The browser's API base URL uses HTTPS, which encrypts the connection while it travels between the browser and the API endpoint.

🎫

Bearer authentication

Protected API requests include the current access token in an Authorization header rather than putting the token in the visible URL.

🚫

No browser credentials

The main generated API client uses credentials: omit and CORS mode for its requests. This means it does not automatically attach ordinary browser cookies to those API calls.

🧩

Different services

Pulse uses separate API services for different features. That means a privacy explanation should not pretend that every request follows one identical path.

Your privacy controls

These controls are in Pulse Settings → Privacy & Safety.

Appear in people search

When disabled, your account is excluded from the user-search results used by Pulse. This does not mean that every possible route to an account disappears; it specifically controls user discovery/search.

Search-only messaging

When enabled, Pulse can require someone to find your account through search before starting a conversation. This is a contact-control feature, not an invisibility guarantee.

Blocked accounts

You can view and manage accounts you have blocked. Blocking is intended to prevent unwanted contact and gives you a direct safety control.

Delete account

Pulse provides an account deletion flow from Settings. It clears the browser session after a successful deletion request. Server-side deletion and any retention that may be required by law or backups should not be assumed to happen instantly unless the service explicitly confirms it.

School & student safety

Pulse is designed to respect school privacy, not to help students bypass school controls.

🏫

During school hours

The current privacy screen runs Monday–Friday during the configured 8:30 AM–3:30 PM window. Weekends are not blocked by this feature.

📡

No Wi-Fi check

The gate does not ask Pulse which school network you are using. This avoids collecting or depending on school-network identity for the privacy screen.

🧑‍🏫

School networks are separate

A school can independently filter websites or network traffic. Pulse does not promise to bypass those controls. If a school network blocks a service, follow the school's rules and speak to school IT or administration if access is legitimately needed.

🤝

Student-to-student safety

Do not use Pulse to bully, threaten, pressure, impersonate, stalk or repeatedly contact another student after they ask you to stop.

Important: The school-hours screen is a privacy measure, not a school IT enforcement system. Do not use VPNs, proxies or other tools to bypass managed school-network restrictions.

How safe is the school-hours screen?

The screen is useful, but it is deliberately simple.

It does not know where you are. Pulse checks the browser's local date and time. It does not need to know your school, Wi-Fi name or network address to decide whether to show the screen.
It is not tamper-proof. Because the check happens in the browser, someone who controls their device can potentially change the device clock or otherwise modify the client. It should therefore never be described as a guaranteed technical barrier.
Why use it anyway? It provides a simple privacy-respecting default during normal school use without requiring Pulse to identify a student's network or physical location.

How safe is Pulse overall?

The honest answer is: Pulse has meaningful privacy and safety controls, but no online service can promise perfect safety.

  • Use a strong password that you do not reuse elsewhere.
  • Never give your password, access token, refresh token or authentication code to another person.
  • Check the recipient before sending something private.
  • Do not put your home address, school login, phone number, payment information or passwords in messages.
  • Sign out on shared computers.
  • Use discoverability and search-only messaging settings if you want more control over who can find/contact you.
  • Block people who make you uncomfortable and report serious problems.
  • If something online makes you feel unsafe, tell a trusted adult, parent/guardian, teacher or school wellbeing staff member.

What Pulse does not claim

Being honest about limitations is part of privacy.

No perfect-security promisePulse is a developing web application. We do not claim that it is immune to bugs, compromised accounts, device malware, network monitoring, administrator access, future vulnerabilities or mistakes.
No location promiseThe school-hours feature does not determine your physical location. Other parts of the internet may still expose ordinary connection information to the services you connect to.
No absolute deletion promiseAccount deletion is available in the app, but this page does not promise instant destruction of every copy that could exist in backups, logs or legally required records.
No bypass promisePulse should not be used to evade school filtering, device management or other access controls.

If something goes wrong

Use the Pulse Help Center's report page for bugs or privacy concerns. For a serious student-safety issue, involve a trusted adult or the appropriate school/emergency service instead of relying on Pulse alone.

Never include secrets in a report. Do not send passwords, authentication tokens, recovery codes or payment-card details to support.
