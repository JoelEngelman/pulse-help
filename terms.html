1. Acceptance of these terms

By creating an account, signing in, accessing, or using Pulse, you agree to follow these Terms & Conditions and the Pulse Community Standards on this page. If you do not agree with them, do not use Pulse.

These rules apply to the Pulse website, applications, messaging features, profiles, social features, support pages, and other services operated as part of Pulse, unless a separate written agreement says otherwise.

We want the rules to be understandable. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, choose the safer and kinder option. When in doubt, do not post it, do not send it, and do not ask another person to do it for you.

2. Our promise: Pulse should be a kind place

Pulse is built around a simple idea: people should be able to communicate without being afraid of being mocked, threatened, manipulated, sexually targeted, stalked, or deliberately humiliated.

Kindness does not mean everyone must agree. You can disagree, criticise an idea, leave a conversation, block someone, or report behaviour you believe is unsafe. You should do those things without attacking someone because of who they are.

Our standard: Treat the person on the other side of the screen like a real human being. If you would not say something safely and respectfully face-to-face, think carefully before sending it online.

We encourage users to welcome newcomers, give people room to learn, apologise when they make mistakes, and avoid turning disagreements into personal attacks.

3. Accounts and truthful use

3.1 One person, one identity per account

Do not create accounts for the purpose of deceiving people, evading a moderation action, harassing someone, or pretending to be another real person.

3.2 Keep your account secure

You are responsible for protecting your login information. Never give your password to a friend, stranger, moderator, developer, or anyone claiming they need it to fix your account. Pulse support should never need your password.

3.3 Accurate information

Do not deliberately enter information designed to impersonate another person or organisation. A display name can be creative, but it must not be used to make other users reasonably believe you are someone you are not.

3.4 Account sharing

Do not share an account in a way that creates confusion about who is communicating. If you use Pulse on a shared computer, sign out when you are finished.

4. Age, minors and child safety

Pulse must comply with the age requirements that apply to the service and the user's location. If Pulse publishes a minimum age for account creation, you must meet that requirement. If a parent or guardian needs to supervise a younger user, follow the rules and tools Pulse provides for that situation.

Children and teenagers deserve special protection online. Adults must not use Pulse to pursue inappropriate, secretive, romantic, sexual, or exploitative relationships with children.

Zero tolerance: Sexual exploitation of children, grooming, sexual conversations with children, requests for sexual images from minors, sharing sexual images of minors, threats involving sexual material, or attempts to move a child into secret communication are prohibited. Such behaviour may be reported to appropriate authorities where required or appropriate.

Do not ask another user for their school address, home address, private phone number, precise location, passwords, intimate images, or other sensitive information simply because you met them on Pulse.

If you are a young person and someone makes you uncomfortable, asks you to keep secrets, pressures you to send pictures, threatens you, or tries to isolate you from trusted adults, stop communicating if it is safe to do so, block or report the account, and tell a trusted adult.

If you see material involving the sexual exploitation of children, do not download it, save it, forward it, repost it, or share it as “proof.” Use Pulse's reporting process and, where appropriate, contact the relevant child-safety or law-enforcement authority.

5. Community conduct

Pulse users must communicate in good faith. The following behaviours are expected:

  • Be respectful, including during disagreements.
  • Respect another person's decision not to communicate with you.
  • Stop contacting someone when they clearly ask you to stop.
  • Use the block and report tools instead of escalating conflicts.
  • Do not deliberately embarrass, threaten, or intimidate another user.
  • Do not encourage groups of people to target an individual.
  • Do not celebrate or encourage violence against people.
  • Do not use Pulse to organise harassment outside Pulse.

Being rude once is not the same as running a sustained harassment campaign. Context matters. Nevertheless, repeated behaviour that makes another person reasonably feel unsafe can violate these rules even if each individual message appears small when viewed alone.

6. Prohibited content and behaviour

You may not use Pulse for content or conduct that is illegal, abusive, exploitative, or seriously harmful. Prohibited behaviour includes, but is not limited to:

  • Credible threats of violence or encouragement of violence.
  • Sexual exploitation or sexual abuse.
  • Child sexual abuse material or grooming.
  • Non-consensual intimate imagery or threats to publish it.
  • Blackmail, extortion, or coercion.
  • Stalking, doxxing, or targeted tracking of another person.
  • Persistent unwanted contact after someone asks you to stop.
  • Fraud, phishing, credential theft, or scams.
  • Malware, malicious links, or attempts to compromise another person's device.
  • Hate-based attacks or dehumanising targeted abuse.
  • Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual messages.
  • Content that deliberately exposes another person's private information.
  • Impersonation intended to deceive, defraud, harass, or harm.
  • Spam or automated abuse that disrupts the service.

Some subjects may be discussed for education, news, safety, moderation, or support. Context does not automatically make otherwise harmful material acceptable. Do not use “it was a joke” or “I was testing the rules” as a reason to send prohibited material.

7. Grooming, sexual exploitation and protecting children

Grooming can involve building trust with a child or teenager for the purpose of sexual exploitation or another form of abuse. It can include secrecy, gifts, manipulation, sexualised conversations, requests for images, gradual boundary pushing, threats, or attempts to move the conversation to a private channel.

Pulse prohibits attempts to groom or sexually exploit anyone, especially children. You must not:

  • Ask a minor for nude or sexual images.
  • Send sexual material to a minor.
  • Offer money, gifts, game items, or favours in exchange for sexual material or sexual contact.
  • Encourage a minor to hide communications from parents, guardians, teachers, or other trusted adults.
  • Arrange or encourage a sexual meeting with a minor.
  • Threaten to reveal private material unless a minor complies with a request.
  • Use an alternate account to bypass a block or safety restriction involving a child.
Never test this boundary. If you think a message might cross a child-safety line, do not send it. Report suspicious behaviour instead.

8. Harassment, bullying and unwanted contact

Users have the right to leave conversations. If someone blocks you, asks you to stop, or otherwise clearly communicates that they do not want contact, respect that boundary.

Do not create another account to get around a block. Do not recruit friends to message someone after they have asked for no contact. Do not repeatedly mention someone in public posts to provoke them. Do not publish their personal information to encourage strangers to contact them.

Constructive criticism is allowed. Personal abuse, threats, intimidation, humiliation campaigns, and targeted harassment are not.

9. Privacy and personal information

Respect other people's privacy. Do not publish or send someone's home address, private phone number, private email address, passwords, financial details, school details, precise live location, medical information, private photographs, or other sensitive information without a legitimate reason and appropriate permission.

Do not use information visible on Pulse to investigate or track someone offline. A public profile does not mean that every possible use of the information is welcome.

Likewise, protect your own privacy. Think before sharing information that could identify where you live, where you go to school, where you are right now, or how someone could contact you away from Pulse.

10. Messaging standards

Pulse messaging is intended for genuine communication. You may message someone because you want to talk to them, collaborate, ask a reasonable question, or otherwise communicate appropriately.

Do not use messaging to spam hundreds of people, repeatedly contact people who do not want contact, send malicious links, impersonate support staff, request passwords, distribute scams, or pressure someone into sharing private information.

A recipient's ability to receive a message is not permission to keep sending messages. Consent and context matter.

11. User discovery and search

Pulse is designed so users are not presented with a giant public directory of everyone using the service. People can search for an account by username or display name when they have a reason to find that person.

Search results may include close matches rather than requiring a perfect spelling match. This is intended to make finding friends easier while still avoiding a passive “show me everyone” directory.

Do not abuse search to identify, catalogue, monitor, or target people. Automated scraping, bulk enumeration, or attempts to build an external database of Pulse users are prohibited.

Do not assume that because you found a person through search they want to talk to you. Finding someone and receiving permission to communicate with them are different things.

12. Identity, impersonation and deception

You may use a nickname, stage name, gaming name, or other display identity, provided it is not used to deceive or harm people.

Do not pretend to be a real person, Pulse employee, moderator, emergency service, school, business, celebrity, or other organisation when that false identity could reasonably mislead people.

Do not create fake screenshots, fake moderation messages, or fake Pulse support conversations to trick another user.

13. Security

Never attempt to gain unauthorised access to another user's account, Pulse's infrastructure, another user's device, or a service connected to Pulse.

Do not probe for passwords, session cookies, authentication tokens, private API endpoints, database credentials, or other secrets. Do not distribute malware, credential-stealing pages, exploit code intended to harm Pulse users, or instructions designed to compromise another person's account.

Good-faith security research should be reported privately through an appropriate security contact rather than used against real users.

14. Reporting problems and unsafe behaviour

If someone breaks these rules, use Pulse's reporting tools or the Pulse Help Center's reporting page when available. Give enough information for the moderation team to understand what happened.

Do not exaggerate a report deliberately. Do not coordinate false reports against someone because you dislike them. A report should describe behaviour, not simply say “I don't like this person.”

For urgent danger, immediate threats to someone's life, or situations requiring emergency intervention, contact the appropriate emergency service in your location rather than relying on Pulse moderation alone.

For child-safety emergencies, involve a trusted adult and the appropriate child-safety or law-enforcement authority. Do not confront a suspected offender yourself if doing so could increase risk.

15. Moderation and enforcement

Pulse may review reports and take reasonable action to protect the community. Depending on the circumstances, action can include removing content, limiting features, warning an account, temporarily suspending an account, permanently banning an account, or referring serious matters to appropriate authorities.

We aim for moderation decisions to be proportionate and based on evidence. However, no moderation system is perfect. Reports may be incomplete, context may be unclear, and automated systems can make mistakes.

Do not attempt to evade a moderation action by creating new accounts or manipulating the service. If an appeal process is provided, use that process.

Pulse may take stronger action where there is a credible safety risk, serious exploitation, repeated harmful conduct, or an attempt to evade previous enforcement.

16. User-generated content

You retain rights you already have in content you create. By uploading or sending content through Pulse, you give Pulse the permissions reasonably necessary to operate the service, such as storing, transmitting, displaying, and processing that content for its intended purpose.

You are responsible for having the right to share material you upload. Do not upload material you stole, do not have permission to distribute, or that violates another person's rights.

Do not use another person's private photograph, recording, writing, artwork, or other material in a way that violates their rights or privacy.

17. Copyright and intellectual property

Do not use Pulse to deliberately distribute copyrighted material without permission or a lawful basis. If you believe your copyright has been infringed, use the applicable reporting process.

Pulse names, branding, logos, software, design, and original Help Center materials may belong to their respective owners. Do not copy Pulse's branding in a way that makes another service appear to be the official Pulse service.

18. Bots, scraping and automation

Automation must not be used to harm the service or its users. In particular, do not scrape user directories, enumerate accounts at scale, send mass unsolicited messages, create thousands of fake accounts, manipulate engagement, or repeatedly hit endpoints in a way that degrades service.

Future official APIs may have their own rules, quotas, and authentication requirements. Follow those requirements rather than trying to imitate a browser or bypass limits.

19. Platform availability

Pulse is provided as a developing service. Features may change, temporarily stop working, be unavailable in a particular browser or network, or be retired. We will try to communicate important changes where practical, but uninterrupted availability cannot be promised.

Do not rely on Pulse as your only method of contacting emergency services, storing irreplaceable information, or maintaining critical communications.

20. Suspension, termination and leaving Pulse

You may stop using Pulse at any time. If account deletion is available, use the account settings or support process provided.

Pulse may suspend or terminate accounts that seriously or repeatedly violate these terms, create security or safety risks, engage in illegal activity, or attempt to evade enforcement.

If an account is removed, do not immediately create replacement accounts to continue the prohibited conduct. If you believe an action was incorrect, use the available appeal process.

21. Changes to these terms

As Pulse develops, these terms may need to change. Important changes should be communicated in a reasonable way. Continuing to use Pulse after an updated version becomes effective means you accept the updated terms, subject to applicable law.

The date at the top of this page indicates the most recent revision.

22. Contact and support

For ordinary product problems, use the Pulse Help Center. For safety reports, use the reporting tools and provide clear information. Never send a password, authentication token, payment-card number, or other secret as part of a support request.

If you are reporting a serious safety issue, describe what happened, when it happened, which account was involved if known, and any relevant message or report identifier. Avoid redistributing harmful material just to provide evidence.

23. The Pulse principles

These principles summarise the culture we want Pulse to have:

  1. Kindness first. There is a human being behind every account.
  2. Respect boundaries. “No,” “stop,” and “leave me alone” are complete answers.
  3. Protect young people. Children and teenagers deserve strong protection from exploitation and manipulation.
  4. Don't hunt people. Finding a username is not permission to stalk, catalogue, or track someone.
  5. Don't weaponise private information. Personal information should not become a tool for intimidation.
  6. Disagree without cruelty. Ideas can be challenged without attacking the person.
  7. Report instead of escalating. If something is unsafe, use the tools designed to handle it.
  8. Own your mistakes. Apologise, correct misinformation, and learn.
  9. Protect your account. Passwords and security tokens belong to you and should stay secret.
  10. Leave room for people. Not every message needs an answer, and not every person needs to become your friend.
The Pulse rule: If your goal is to make someone feel welcome, understood, safe, or respected, you're probably moving in the right direction. If your goal is to frighten, manipulate, exploit, expose, humiliate, or control someone, stop.

These standards are intended to make Pulse a place where people can communicate, make friends, share ideas, and have fun without sacrificing basic human dignity.

Final acknowledgement

Pulse is a community, not a hunting ground. Use the service responsibly. Protect yourself and other people. Respect boundaries. Speak up when something is unsafe. And, above all, be kind.
