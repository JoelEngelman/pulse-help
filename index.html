NEW TO PULSE?

Start with the basics.

Go from your first sign-in to your first conversation with a simple walkthrough.

Welcome to Pulse. The basic workflow is simple: sign in, make sure your profile is ready, find the person you want to talk to, and open a conversation.

1. Sign in or create your account

If you already have a Pulse account, sign in with your account details. If you are new to Pulse, use the account creation flow and complete the required information.

2. Check your profile

Open your profile and make sure your name and other visible information are correct. Keeping your profile up to date makes it easier for other people to recognise the right account.

3. Find a person

Use Pulse's account or messaging interface to find the account you want to contact. Double-check the account name before starting a private conversation.

4. Send your first message

Open the conversation, type your message and press Send. The conversation should then be available from both sides of the messaging system.

Tip: If something does not appear immediately, refresh the conversation once before assuming the message or account was lost.

Account & profile

Detailed account guides. Each article expands in place so you can read the whole answer without leaving the page.

Creating an account starts with the Pulse sign-up screen. Enter the information requested by the app and submit the form once. After the account is created, you should be able to sign in and continue to your profile.

Before you start

Use an email address or account identifier that you can access and choose a password you will remember. Do not use somebody else's account details, and never send your password to another person for help.

If account creation fails

Read the exact error message first. A temporary connection problem can sometimes be fixed by refreshing the page and trying again. Avoid clicking the create-account button repeatedly, because repeated submissions can make an existing account look like a duplicate.

If you receive a database or duplicate-account error even though you believe the account is new, stop retrying and report the exact error. Include what you entered only when it is safe to do so; never include your password.

If you use more than one Pulse account, the important thing is to make sure you know which account is currently signed in before sending messages or changing settings.

Switching accounts

  1. Open the account or profile controls.
  2. Sign out of the current account if an account switcher is not available.
  3. Choose Sign In and enter the other account's details.
  4. After signing in, check the profile or account name to confirm you are using the intended account.

If the old account keeps appearing, refresh Pulse after signing out. If you use a shared computer, signing out completely is especially important so another person cannot accidentally use your session.

Tip: Before sending a private message, check the active account one more time. This prevents messages being sent from the wrong profile.

Password changes should be made from the account settings area. Look for a password, security or account settings option and follow the form shown there.

  1. Open your account settings.
  2. Choose the password or security option.
  3. Enter your current password if Pulse asks for it.
  4. Enter the new password and confirm it.
  5. Save the change and wait for the confirmation.

Choose a password that is difficult for other people to guess. Avoid using your name, birthday, school name or another easily known detail. Do not reuse an important password from another service.

If Pulse says the current password is incorrect, do not keep guessing. Check that you are signed into the correct account and use the account's recovery process if one is available.

For security, a properly designed password system should not display your existing password in plain text. A password visibility button can reveal the characters you are currently typing into a password field, but it cannot normally recover a password that has already been stored.

If you have forgotten your password, use the available password-reset or account-recovery process instead of trying to reveal the stored password.

Security note: Anyone asking you to send them your password so they can “fix” Pulse is not following a safe support process. Never share it.

Your profile is the information other Pulse users may see when they interact with your account. Open the profile or account settings area and choose the edit option.

  1. Open your profile.
  2. Choose Edit or the relevant profile setting.
  3. Change the information you want to update.
  4. Review the changes carefully.
  5. Save the profile.

After saving, return to the profile view and confirm that the new information appears. If it does not, refresh the page once. If the old information continues to appear after a successful save, the issue may be a server or caching problem and should be reported.

Sign-in failures can come from an incorrect password, the wrong account, a temporary network problem, or an account that has not actually been created.

Try these checks

  1. Make sure you are using the correct account identifier.
  2. Check Caps Lock and make sure the password is entered exactly.
  3. Refresh Pulse and try the sign-in once more.
  4. Check that your internet connection is working.
  5. If you recently changed your password, use the new password.

If the account does not exist, create it before attempting to sign in. If the same error continues, record the exact message and report it rather than repeatedly attempting the same action.

Never share your password. Pulse support should not need your password to troubleshoot an account.

Messaging

Learn how Pulse finds accounts, creates conversations and delivers messages.

Messaging starts by choosing the account you want to contact. Before writing anything private, make sure the conversation belongs to the correct person.

  1. Open the Messaging area.
  2. Search for or choose the recipient.
  3. Open the conversation.
  4. Type your message in the composer.
  5. Press Send and wait for the message to appear in the conversation.

A successful send should result in the message appearing as part of the conversation. If it does not appear, do not immediately send the same message repeatedly because that can make troubleshooting harder.

If a conversation is missing messages, first confirm that you are looking at the correct conversation and are signed into the expected account. An account switch can make it look like a conversation has disappeared when you are actually viewing another profile.

Work through the problem

  1. Check the active account.
  2. Check the recipient name.
  3. Refresh the conversation.
  4. Wait a moment for the latest data to load.
  5. Open the conversation again if necessary.

If the other person can see the message but you cannot, the problem may be with refreshing or retrieving the conversation. If neither side can see it, check whether the send operation actually succeeded and whether the network is working.

Message delivery depends on the account being valid, the recipient being available in Pulse, the frontend being connected to the Pulse backend, and the network allowing the request to reach the service.

Check each part

  1. Confirm the recipient account exists.
  2. Confirm you are signed into the correct account.
  3. Make sure the message box contains text.
  4. Check your connection.
  5. Refresh Pulse and try one test message.

If messages still fail, note whether the Send button does anything, whether an error appears, and whether the message briefly appears and then disappears. Those details help identify whether the problem is in the browser, network or backend.

Pulse messaging is intended to work in both directions: one account sends a message and the recipient's conversation can retrieve it. Both accounts should therefore be able to participate in the same conversation without manually copying messages between devices.

If Account A can send but Account B cannot see the message, check both sessions separately. Refresh Account B's conversation and make sure it is signed into the intended account. If Account B can reply but Account A cannot see the reply, repeat the same checks on Account A.

When both sides consistently fail to see new messages, the problem is more likely to be a connection, API or data-refresh issue than a simple typing problem. Record what each side can see and report it.

Troubleshooting

Detailed fixes for the problems you are most likely to encounter.

If Pulse does not load, first determine whether the problem is the page itself, your browser, your internet connection or the network you are using.

  1. Refresh the page once.
  2. Check whether other websites load normally.
  3. Try a current browser such as Edge, Chrome, Firefox or Safari.
  4. Close the tab and open Pulse again.
  5. If you are on a school or managed network, check whether the network is restricting the service.

If the Pulse page loads but its data does not, that is a different problem from the entire site being unreachable. In that case, look at the account, messaging and database guides below.

A database error means Pulse's application was unable to complete an operation involving stored account or application data. It does not automatically mean that you did something wrong.

For account creation problems, avoid repeatedly pressing Create Account. Refresh the page and make one careful attempt. If the error mentions a duplicate key, an existing profile or another database constraint, the account may already have been partially created and repeated attempts can make the situation more confusing.

What to report

Copy the exact error message, explain which action caused it, and say whether it happens every time. Do not include your password, session information or other private credentials in a report.

At this point, test whether the issue affects one conversation or every conversation. If only one recipient is affected, check that account carefully. If every recipient fails, the problem is more likely to be related to your session, network or the messaging backend.

Refresh Pulse, sign out and back in if appropriate, and send one short test message. Watch what happens after pressing Send. A visible error, a loading state that never finishes, or a message that appears locally but never reaches the other account are three different failure patterns.

When reporting the issue, describe the pattern rather than just saying “messages don't work.” That gives whoever maintains Pulse enough information to investigate the correct part of the system.

School & restricted networks

Understand what you can check when a managed network behaves differently.

Schools and other managed organisations can apply filtering rules to websites, domains, categories and network traffic. A site can therefore work normally at home while being unavailable on a school connection.

Pulse uses ordinary web access over HTTPS, but that does not guarantee that every managed network will allow it. The network administrator controls which destinations and services are permitted.

How to tell whether the network is involved

Check whether other websites work. If Pulse fails only on the managed connection but works on a network where you are permitted to use it, that is useful evidence that the restriction is network-specific.

Do not attempt to bypass school security controls. If Pulse is needed for an approved purpose, ask the network administrator or school IT team whether the site can be reviewed.

Yes. Testing another current browser is a safe way to determine whether a problem is specific to one browser's stored data, settings or compatibility.

Try a normal current version of Edge, Chrome, Firefox or Safari. If Pulse works in one browser but not another on the same network, the browser difference is an important clue.

If it fails in every browser but other websites work, the problem is less likely to be a browser-specific issue.

If Pulse is unavailable only on a particular managed network, the correct solution is to have the network policy reviewed by the organisation responsible for it.

Gather simple evidence: the Pulse address you tried, what the browser displayed, whether other sites worked, and whether the same Pulse page worked on another permitted connection. Give that information to the network administrator.

Important: Do not use proxies, VPNs or other methods to evade a school's access controls. Network restrictions exist for a reason, and the administrator is the right person to decide whether Pulse should be allowed.

Privacy & safety

Good account security starts with a few simple habits.

Your password is the key to your account, so treat it as private information. Use a strong password, avoid reusing an important password from another service, and never send it to somebody through Pulse messages, email or chat.

Be careful with screenshots and screen sharing too. A password can accidentally become visible even when you did not intend to send it. If you think somebody else has learned your password, change it as soon as possible using the account's password settings.

Account names can be similar, so check the recipient carefully before sending anything private or sensitive. Look at the profile information available to you and make sure it matches the person you intended to contact.

If you are unsure, ask the person through a trusted channel to confirm their Pulse account name. Once a private message has been sent, you should assume the recipient may be able to read it.

A useful support report explains exactly what happened without exposing private credentials. Start by describing the action you were attempting, such as signing in, creating an account or sending a message.

Include

  • The page or feature you were using.
  • The exact error message, if one appeared.
  • What you expected to happen.
  • What actually happened.
  • Whether refreshing or trying again changed anything.
  • Whether the problem affects one account or multiple accounts.

Never include your password, authentication tokens or other private credentials. A support person should be able to investigate the problem without receiving your secret login information.
